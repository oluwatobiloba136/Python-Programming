## 2. Business Overview and Current Snapshot

Berger Paints Nigeria Plc is a specialist paints and coatings manufacturer serving Nigeria’s decorative, industrial, automotive and marine segments. It operates a factory‑driven model with a national distribution footprint and a portfolio spanning premium and mass‑market brands. The business is NAIRA‑based on both the revenue and cost side, but raw‑material inputs expose it to FX and commodity shocks.

From the overview and statistics pages, Berger trades in the high‑teens naira per share, with roughly 290 million shares outstanding and a market capitalization in the high single‑digit to low‑double‑digit billions of naira. The statistics page shows total market cap around the mid‑single‑digit billions at earlier points, rising toward c. 19–20 billion naira as of early 2026, reflecting a material re‑rating over the last few years as earnings and dividends improved.

The latest trailing‑twelve‑month (TTM) revenue and net income figures support a positive earnings profile, with EPS that has grown relative to pre‑2023 levels. The 52‑week range has seen solid appreciation, and the stock currently trades closer to the upper half of its 52‑week band, indicating constructive market sentiment rather than distress or deep value.

## 3. Growth: Revenue, Earnings and Margins

### 3.1 Revenue Trend

The revenue history shows a multi‑year step‑up in Berger’s scale: revenue has increased across 2020–2024 with very few outright contractions, producing a positive multi‑year CAGR. The market‑cap history confirms that investors have rewarded this growth, with market cap compounding strongly from low single‑digit billions in 2022 to mid/high‑teens billions more recently.

On a one‑year basis, the latest 52‑week price change is positive, while revenue growth remains mid‑single to high‑single digit. That combination suggests a business that is not a hyper‑growth story, but a steady, scaling industrial with room to pass through some inflation and FX cost pressure.

### 3.2 Earnings and Margins

Income‑statement and statistics data show that Berger’s gross margin is healthy for a paints manufacturer, supporting operating and net margins that are positive and reasonably stable. Over the last few years, net profit has grown faster than revenue in several periods, as scale, product mix and cost control have allowed some degree of operating leverage.

Net profit margin sits in the high‑single‑digit to low‑teens band rather than at commodity‑like levels. This reflects the semi‑branded nature of the paints business, where brand and distribution give some pricing power, though FX‑driven input costs and competitive pressure cap how far margins can expand.

The ratios page reports respectable returns:

## 5. Balance Sheet Quality and Leverage

The balance‑sheet page is a key strength of the Berger story. Latest data show:

- Total assets around ₦7.5 billion in 2024, up from about ₦5.1 billion in 2021.  
- Total liabilities around ₦3.7 billion, with shareholders’ equity around ₦3.8 billion.  
- Total debt around ₦338 million in 2024, significantly smaller than equity.  
- Net cash position (cash plus short‑term investments minus total debt) positive and comfortable, with net cash per share over ₦1.

This translates into a **low D/E ratio** and a **very conservative Debt/EBITDA profile**, meaning that Berger has little financial leverage risk. Net cash gives the company buffer against FX shocks, allows it to fund routine capex and dividends internally, and positions it to invest opportunistically or absorb cost spikes without emergency financing.

Working capital is strongly positive, with current assets comfortably exceeding current liabilities; this supports day‑to‑day liquidity and reduces refinancing and rollover risk in a tight naira‑liquidity environment.

## 6. Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow

The cash‑flow statement shows that Berger consistently generates positive operating cash flow, with periodic investment in property, plant and equipment to maintain and modestly expand capacity. After deducting capex, free cash flow (FCF) is typically positive.

FCF margin, while not spectacular, is adequate for a mature cyclical business, and in several years FCF comfortably exceeds dividend cash outflows. This demonstrates that Berger is not funding its dividend by raising leverage or drawing down cash; instead, it pays distributions from genuine cash generation, leaving some runway for reinvestment.

Where capex temporarily spikes (e.g., strategic plant upgrades or capacity additions), FCF can be temporarily depressed, but the net‑cash position and modest leverage mean this does not push the company into distress.

The dividend page shows a continuing pattern of ordinary dividends with occasional increases. - **Enterprise value:** lower than market cap due to net‑cash status.

- **EV/EBITDA:** relatively low because net cash depresses EV; for a consistently profitable manufacturer, this is a favourable signal.

Earnings yield (inverse P/E) and FCF yield compare well to naira risk‑free yields, especially once you adjust for the company’s low financial risk. The market is not pricing Berger as a growth darling, but more as a **steady compounder** where you are paid via dividends and moderate earnings growth, with downside protection from the balance sheet.

## 11. Key Fundamental Metrics – Concepts and Berger‑Specific Interpretation

Below is a concise conceptual view, mapping each core metric to Berger’s profile:

### Net Profit Margin

**Definition:** Net Income / Revenue.  
**Berger:** Net margins in the high‑single‑digit to low‑teens band across recent years.  
**Interpretation:** This level of profitability is solid for a paints manufacturer, showing that Berger converts a meaningful share of revenue into bottom‑line earnings despite FX, input‑cost and competitive pressures.

### Gross Margin

**Definition:** (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue.  
**Berger:** Healthy gross margins, stable over time.  
**Interpretation:** Stable gross margins indicate that Berger has enough pricing power and procurement discipline to pass through part of cost inflation and defend profitability.

### ROE

**Definition:** Net Income / Equity.  
**Berger:** Value‑creating ROE comfortably above low double digits.  
**Interpretation:** Berger is enhancing shareholder value; each naira of equity earns returns higher than the likely naira cost of equity, justifying a P/B above 1x.

### ROA and ROCE

**Definition:** ROA = Net Income / Assets; ROCE ≈ EBIT / (Assets − Current Liabilities).  
**Berger:** ROA in mid‑single digits; ROCE clearly above funding costs.  
**Interpretation:** The company uses its asset base and capital efficiently, with ROCE particularly confirming that capex and working capital produce returns above the cost of capital.

### EPS and EPS Growth

**Definition:** EPS = Net Income / Shares Outstanding.  
**Berger:** EPS has trended upwards over the last few years alongside revenue and net income.  
**Interpretation:** Berger is not just growing the top line, but driving per‑share earnings, protected from dilution.

### P/E, P/B, P/S

**Berger:** Mid‑single‑ to low‑double‑digit P/E; modest premium to book; low sales multiple.  
**Interpretation:** Valuation reflects a **quality, income‑oriented cyclical** rather than a speculative growth play. Investors are paying a modest multiple for a strong balance sheet, positive ROE and dividend.

### Dividend Yield and Payout Ratio

**Definition:** Yield = DPS / Price; Payout = DPS / EPS.  
**Berger:** Attractive dividend yield, moderate payout ratio.  
**Interpretation:** The dividend is meaningful and likely sustainable; Berger retains enough earnings to continue funding capex and balance‑sheet strength.

### FCF, FCF Margin, FCF Yield

**Definition:** FCF = OCF − Capex.  
**Berger:** Positive FCF in most years with reasonable FCF margin and yield.  
**Interpretation:** Free cash flow is sufficient to cover dividends and small organic investments, making Berger a credible long‑term income name.

### D/E, Debt/EBITDA, Interest Coverage

**Berger:** Low debt‑to‑equity, low Debt/EBITDA, strong interest cover, net‑cash position.  
**Interpretation:** Financial risk is low; the company is resilient to spikes in interest rates and downturns, and has capacity to manage shocks.

### Liquidity and Efficiency

**Berger:** Current ratio comfortably above 1, positive working capital, improving inventory and asset turnover.  
**Interpretation:** Short‑term liquidity is sound; Berger can meet obligations and run operations without strain, while gradually improving how capital is employed.

### EV / EBITDA

**Definition:** (Market Cap + Debt − Cash) / EBITDA.  
**Berger:** Low to moderate EV/EBITDA given net cash and steady EBITDA.  
**Interpretation:** Investors are paying a modest multiple of underlying operating cash generation for a stable business with good governance and dividends, which tilts towards undervaluation or at least fair value with an income premium.

## 12. Valuation Conclusion: Undervalued, Overvalued, or Fair

Putting the pieces together:

- Profitability: respectable ROE, healthy gross and net margins.  
- Growth: sustained revenue and EPS growth over several years, with recent acceleration in market cap.  
- Balance sheet: net‑cash, low leverage, strong working capital.  
- Cash flow: positive FCF with dividends largely covered.  
- Governance: consistent reporting, regular dividends and clear board actions.  
- Valuation: moderate P/E, low EV/EBITDA, modest premium to book, attractive dividend and FCF yields.

On this combination, Berger Paints Nigeria Plc appears **modestly undervalued to fairly valued**, skewing towards **undervalued** for long‑term investors who can tolerate Nigeria and sector risk. The market does not seem to be over‑paying for blue‑sky growth; instead, it offers a reasonable price for a quality, cash‑generative business with an excellent balance sheet.

A genuine “overvalued” call would require significantly higher multiples or clear deterioration in returns or governance, which are not evident in the current data.

## 13. Guidance for Long‑Term Investors

For a long‑term fundamental investor:

- **Stance:** **Accumulate / Hold‑and‑add‑on‑weakness.**  
- **Rationale:** Solid profitability, strong balance sheet, positive FCF and a sustainable dividend make Berger a candidate for a core NGX industrial holding.

**When to Buy**

- On pullbacks into the lower half of the 52‑week trading range, especially when driven by broad market risk‑off rather than Berger‑specific deterioration.  
- After strong results where share‑price reaction is muted, creating a temporary mismatch between fundamentals and price.

**When to Hold**

- As long as ROE, margins and FCF remain healthy and leverage stays low, even if valuation migrates from “cheap” to “fair.”  
- When the dividend remains well‑covered by earnings and FCF.

**When to Consider Trimming or Exiting**

- Sustained decline in margins and ROE, suggesting structural profitability erosion.  
- Sharp rise in leverage (D/E and Debt/EBITDA) without clear high‑return projects to justify it.  
- A re‑rating to rich valuation (e.g., high‑teens P/E and elevated EV/EBITDA) without corresponding growth, especially if dividend cover weakens.

## 14. Guidance for Short‑Term Traders

For tactical traders:

- **Bias:** Constructively bullish, with a focus on **buy‑the‑dip and breakout** strategies rather than shorting, given the fundamental backdrop and balance sheet strength.  

**Trading Approach**

- Use recent swing lows and support zones from the price‑history chart as entry zones for long positions on pullbacks, particularly when broader NGX volatility drives indiscriminate selling.  
- Trade breakouts above recent resistance when supported by rising volume, aligning with broader market risk‑on periods and company‑specific catalysts (results, dividend declarations).  

**Risk Management**

- Position size assuming occasional sharp daily moves in response to macro or company news.  
- Avoid outsized leverage ahead of binary events (earnings, major FX policy announcements) where gap risk is high.  
- Respect technical breaks of key support as indications to step aside and wait for clarity, even if the long‑term fundamental story remains intact.
