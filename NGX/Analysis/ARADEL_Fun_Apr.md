## 2. Business Model & Qualitative Overview

### 2.1 Core Business

Aradel is an **integrated energy company** operating across **upstream, midstream, downstream, and energy infrastructure**.[https://www.aradel.com/about-us/our-business/][web:379]  
It produces oil and gas (upstream), processes gas (midstream), refines crude and markets refined products (downstream), and invests in energy assets and associated companies (notably ND Western).[web:379][web:369][web:385]

Revenue is earned from:[web:396][web:383]  
- **Crude oil exports**.  
- **Refined product sales** (from its modular refinery).  
- **Gas sales** (domestic gas supply).  
- **Equity income** from associates and infrastructure investments.  

Revenue is **recurring but cyclical**, driven by production volumes, realised oil/gas prices, refinery margins, and FX.

### 2.2 Industry & Competitive Landscape

Aradel operates in Nigeria’s **independent oil & gas** space, competing with companies like **Seplat Energy**, other marginal‑field operators, and mid‑stream gas and refining players.[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aradel_Holdings][web:372]  
Its advantage is a **fully integrated value chain**: upstream reserves, gas processing, refining, and equity stakes in ND Western; this provides **diversified cash flows** vs single‑segment peers.[web:381][web:385]  

Key competitive edges:[web:381][web:386][web:383]  
- Early mover in marginal fields (Ogbele).  
- Investment in **modular refining** and gas infrastructure.  
- Strong association with institutional investor **African Capital Alliance** (ACA).

### ARADEL – Simple DDM/DCF Framework

Inputs (example – update with your own assumptions):
- Base EPS (last FY): EPS₀ = ₦59.35
- Base DPS (last FY total): DPS₀ = ₦30
- Near‑term EPS growth (yrs 1–5): g₁ = 10–15%
- Long‑term EPS growth (after yr 5): g₂ = 3–4%
- Target payout ratio: p = 35–40%
- Cost of equity: r = 18–22%
- Current price: P₀ ≈ ₦1,200

Steps:
1. Forecast EPS₁…EPS₅:
   EPS_t = EPS₀ × (1 + g₁)^t

2. Forecast DPS₁…DPS₅:
   DPS_t = p × EPS_t

3. Compute terminal value at year 5:
   Option A (P/E exit):
   TV₅ = EPS₆ × Exit P/E (e.g. 8–12x),
   where EPS₆ = EPS₅ × (1 + g₂).

   Option B (DDM):
   DPS₆ = DPS₅ × (1 + g₂)
   TV₅ = DPS₆ / (r − g₂)

4. Discount all DPS_t and TV₅ at r:
   PV = Σ [DPS_t / (1 + r)^t] + TV₅ / (1 + r)^5

5. Compare PV per share vs P₀:
   - PV >> P₀ by 20–30% → **Buy/Accumulate**
   - PV ≈ P₀ → **Hold**
   - PV << P₀ → **Reduce/Sell** unless non‑quantitative thesis is strong.

## 11. Glossary of Abbreviations

- **ROE:** Return on Equity – net income ÷ shareholders’ equity; high ROE suggests efficient use of capital.  
- **ROA:** Return on Assets – net income ÷ total assets; shows profitability per unit of assets.  
- **P/E:** Price‑to‑Earnings – share price ÷ earnings per share; lower can indicate cheaper valuation.  
- **P/B:** Price‑to‑Book – share price ÷ book value per share; relates price to net asset value.  
- **P/S:** Price‑to‑Sales – market cap ÷ revenue; rough revenue‑based valuation.  
- **EBITDA:** Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation & amortisation; proxy for operating cash generation.  
- **D/E:** Debt‑to‑Equity – total debt ÷ equity; leverage indicator.  
- **OCF:** Operating Cash Flow – cash generated from operations.  
- **FCF:** Free Cash Flow – OCF minus capex; cash available for dividends, buybacks, or debt reduction.  
- **DCF:** Discounted Cash Flow – valuation by discounting future cash flows.  
- **DDM:** Dividend Discount Model – valuation by discounting expected dividends.  
- **EPS:** Earnings Per Share – net income ÷ shares outstanding.  
- **DPS:** Dividend Per Share – total dividend ÷ shares outstanding.
