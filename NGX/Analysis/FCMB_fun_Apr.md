## 1. Business Model & Qualitative Overview

### 1.1 Core Business

FCMB Group Plc is a Nigerian financial services holding company with operations in:

- **Commercial & retail banking** – loans, deposits, payments.  
- **Investment banking & capital markets** – corporate finance, advisory.  
- **Asset & wealth management and pensions** – fee‑based savings and investment products.  
- **International banking (UK)** – FCMB Bank UK.

It earns mainly through:

- **Net interest income** (loan interest minus funding costs).  
- **Non‑interest income** – fees, commissions, trading income, asset‑management and pension fees.

Revenue is **recurring but cyclical**, sensitive to Nigeria’s credit cycle, interest rates and FX.

### 1.2 Industry & Competitive Position

FCMB competes in the **Nigerian banking sector**, below the largest Tier‑1 names (GTCO, Zenith, Access, UBA), but with:

- A strong **SME and retail** footprint.  
- Growing **wealth/pension** and **digital** businesses.  

Its competitive edge is more in **customer niches and innovation** than in sheer scale. Moat quality is **moderate**: recognisable brand and SME franchise, but not as dominant as GTCO/Zenith.

### 1.3 Leadership & Governance (High Level)

- Long operating history with evolving strategy (from traditional banking to diversified group).  
- Recent years show **improving execution** and more consistent earnings.  
- Governance is typical of Nigerian listed banks: independent directors, committees, regulatory oversight. No major new governance scandals have dominated recent coverage, but it is not a standout governance benchmark either.

### 1.4 Growth Drivers & Risks

**Growth drivers**

- **Earnings momentum** – multi‑year growth in profit after tax, supported by higher rates, loan growth and fee income.  
- **Digital banking & innovation** – mobile and online platforms, digital lending and payments.  
- **SME & retail expansion** – large under‑served segments with long runway.  
- **Wealth & pensions** – recurring, capital‑light fee income.

**Key risks**

- **Nigeria macro risk** – inflation, FX volatility, regulatory changes, political risk.  
- **Credit risk** – SME/retail lending is higher‑yielding but more vulnerable in downturns.  
- **Capital & leverage** – smaller scale and higher leverage than top‑tier peers; recapitalisation cycles matter.

### 3.1 Income Statement (3–5 Year Trend)

Key observations:

- **Gross earnings** and **net income** have grown strongly over the last 3–5 years.  
- In the most recent year(s), **profit before tax (PBT)** and **profit after tax (PAT)** have shown **very strong double‑digit growth**, indicating operating leverage and a favourable rate environment.  
- Net interest income has benefited from higher yields, while non‑interest income (fees, trading, asset management) adds diversification.

You should:

- Calculate revenue and PAT **CAGR** over 3–5 years.  
- Track margins:
  - PBT margin = PBT / gross earnings.  
  - PAT margin = PAT / gross earnings.  
- Highlight whether margins are **expanding or contracting**.

### 3.2 Balance Sheet

- Total assets, loans and deposits have expanded steadily.  
- Leverage (e.g., debt‑to‑equity or equity/assets) is **higher than top‑tier banks**, but not unusual for a growing Nigerian lender.  
- Capital adequacy and liquidity ratios should be checked against regulatory minima.

You should:

- Compute **loan‑to‑deposit ratio (LDR)**.  
- Check **capital adequacy** disclosures.  
- Note any rapid growth in loans that could foreshadow credit issues.

### 3.3 Cash Flow & Earnings Quality

- For banks, cash‑flow statements are harder to interpret in the traditional corporate sense, but you should:
  - Check whether **earnings are recurring** (not dominated by one‑offs).  
  - Compare **multi‑year PAT trends** with changes in **equity and dividends** to ensure retained earnings are building capital.  

Earnings quality looks acceptable if:

- PAT is supported by underlying interest and fee income,  
- Impairments are not chronically suppressed relative to loan growth,  
- There is no pattern of large recurring one‑off gains.

## 6. Macroeconomic & Sector Context

- Nigeria’s environment is **high‑risk, high‑return**:  
  - Elevated inflation and rate volatility.

  - Regulatory and recapitalisation cycles.  
- These factors:
  - **Boost nominal bank earnings**,  
  - But **justify high discount rates** and frequent bouts of market stress.

For FCMB:

- The macro backdrop can **supercharge earnings in “good” periods**, but also **hit SME/retail portfolios** hard in downturns.
